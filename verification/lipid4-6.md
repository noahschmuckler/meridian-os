**Claim 1: Two "automatic" statin-initiation indications — PREVENT ≥10% → high-intensity statin (COR 1) and LDL-C ≥190 → high-intensity statin (COR 1), both without shared decision-making.**

**Partially supported but with significant errors.** The 2026 guideline does recommend high-intensity statin for PREVENT-ASCVD ≥10% 10-year risk (COR 1, LOE A, Recommendation 7, Section 4.2.3.7) and maximally tolerated statin for LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL (COR 1, Section 4.2.4.3).[1][2] However, the claim that these proceed "without a formal shared-decision-making conversation" is **incorrect**. The 2026 guideline explicitly makes the individualized benefit-risk discussion a core element of patient-centered care at every risk level (Section 4.2.3.1).[2] The guideline states that a "benefit-risk discussion is indicated to determine a decision regarding initiation of LLT" and provides a checklist for this discussion.[1] COR 1 means the treatment "should" be provided — it does not exempt the clinician from shared decision-making. Additionally, the claim frames these as the only two "automatic" indications; **diabetes (ages 40–75) is a third COR 1 statin indication** (moderate-intensity, Section 4.2.5).[2]

Operationally: Document the benefit-risk discussion even for COR 1 indications. For LDL-C ≥190, also exclude secondary causes (hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, ketogenic diet) and evaluate for familial hypercholesterolemia before initiating.[1]

**Claim 2: Borderline-risk (PREVENT 3% to <5%) — SDM applies (COR 2a); statin reasonable when any 2026-table-13 risk-enhancing factor is present.**

[2] The risk enhancers listed in Table 13 of the guideline match the claim well, including Lp(a) ≥125 nmol/L or ≥50 mg/dL, family history of premature ASCVD, hsCRP ≥2 mg/L on >1 occasion, TG persistently ≥175 mg/dL nonfasting or ≥150 mg/dL fasting, higher-risk ancestry (South Asian, Filipino), chronic inflammatory disease, LDL-C 160–189 mg/dL, non-HDL-C 190–219 mg/dL, apoB ≥120 mg/dL, and reproductive risk markers.[2]

**Key additions the claim omits:** The 2026 guideline adds **CKM syndrome** as a new risk enhancer and **high polygenic risk score** (if measured).[2] Also, the hsCRP-specific recommendation for borderline-risk patients is notably stronger than the general risk-enhancer recommendation: hsCRP ≥2 mg/L on 2 successive occasions supports **high-intensity** statin therapy (COR 2a, LOE B-R) — not just moderate-intensity.[2][1]

**Claim 3: Intermediate-risk (PREVENT 5% to <10%), moderate-intensity statin reasonable (COR 2a), LDL-C goal <100.**

[2]

**Correction needed:** The module should state COR 1 for intermediate-risk statin initiation, not COR 2a. This is a clinically meaningful distinction — intermediate-risk patients should receive a statin recommendation, not merely a "reasonable to consider" framing.

**Claim 4: CAC scoring upgraded to COR 1 for risk reclassification in intermediate-risk and select borderline-risk adults (men ≥40, women ≥45).**

**Supported.** The 2026 guideline upgrades CAC scoring from COR 2a (2018) to **COR 1 (LOE B-R)** for risk stratification when the decision regarding LLT remains uncertain in intermediate-risk and select borderline-risk adults (Section 4.2.3.6, Recommendation 1).[2][1] The age thresholds of men ≥40 and women ≥45 are consistent with the guideline's framing of CAC testing as "generally recommended for use in men ≥40 years and women ≥45 years of age."[2]

---

**Claim 5: CAC = 0 supports deferral with reassessment in 3–7 years (not 5–7). CAC ≥100 or ≥75th percentile supports statin initiation.**

**Supported.** The 2026 guideline states it is reasonable to defer therapy and reassess with repeat CAC testing in **3 to 7 years** when CAC = 0 (COR 2a, LOE B-NR, Section 4.2.3.6, Recommendation 2), provided no higher-risk conditions are present (FH, LDL-C ≥190, diabetes age >40, current smoking, strong family history of premature ASCVD).[2][1] The claim correctly identifies that the prior module's "5–7 years" was too narrow.

For CAC >0, the guideline recommends initiating LLT (COR 1, LOE B-NR), "particularly if the CAC score is ≥100 AU or ≥75th standardized percentile." However, the claim slightly oversimplifies:[2] **any CAC >0 now triggers a COR 1 recommendation to initiate LLT**, with CAC ≥100 or ≥75th percentile being the strongest indication within that recommendation.[2]

**Claim 6: CAC ≥1000 → LDL-C goal <55 (COR 1, LOE B-NR). For CAC 300–999, COR 2a to intensify therapy to achieve LDL-C <55.**

[2] For CAC 300–999, the **primary recommendation** is COR 1 (LOE B-R) to achieve LDL-C **<70 mg/dL**.[2] The claim conflates these two tiers. Operationally: for CAC 300–999, the initial goal is <70 mg/dL with intensification to <55 mg/dL being a separate COR 2a sub-recommendation.[2][1]

**Claim 7: 30-year PREVENT-ASCVD ≥10% in adults aged 30–59 with low 10-year risk (<3%) → moderate-intensity statin (Class 2a).**

**Important nuance:** The LOE is **C-LD** (limited data), not B-NR or higher, because no RCTs have been conducted in individuals at <3% 10-year risk despite ≥10% 30-year risk.[1] The guideline explicitly acknowledges this evidence gap. The claim correctly characterizes this as a statin-initiation trigger rather than just a counseling tool, but the module should communicate the lower evidence level and the guideline's emphasis on a "trial of health behavior optimization" before pharmacotherapy.[2]

**Claim 8: CAC scoring cost ~$75–$150 out-of-pocket; CMS/Medicare expanded coverage as of CY 2024.**

**Claim 9: Statin counseling — CTT ~22% RRR per mmol/L, no signal for cancer/dementia/hemorrhagic stroke, NNT ~50–100 for primary prevention.**

**Largely supported with precision corrections.** The CTT Collaboration meta-analysis demonstrated approximately a **20–21% proportional reduction** in major vascular events per 1 mmol/L LDL-C reduction (with ~24% after the first year of treatment).[3][4] The Silverman et al. meta-analysis reported ~23% per mmol/L across LDL-receptor-upregulating therapies.[5] The claim's "~22%" is a reasonable approximation.

Regarding safety, the 2019 AHA Scientific Statement on Statin Safety (Newman et al.) confirmed no causal association between statins and cancer or cognitive impairment.[6] The claim references "Goldstein et al. 2023 AHA ATVB scientific statement" — the correct reference is **Newman et al. 2019 AHA ATVB Scientific Statement**.[6] There is no 2023 "Goldstein et al." AHA ATVB statement on this topic in the available literature.

The NNT range of ~50–100 for primary prevention is a reasonable approximation for intermediate-to-high-risk patients over ~5 years, consistent with CTT data showing 11 fewer major vascular events per 1000 treated over 5 years per 1 mmol/L LDL-C reduction in lower-risk populations.[4]

**Claim 10: Statin-induced diabetes — ~9–12% RRI, ~1 case per 255 patient-years.**

**Supported with updated data.** The 2024 CTT individual participant data meta-analysis found a **10% proportional increase** in new-onset diabetes with low/moderate-intensity statins (RR 1.10, 95% CI 1.04–1.16) and a **36% increase** with high-intensity statins (RR 1.36, 95% CI 1.25–1.48).[7] The ADA Standards of Care 2026 cites the classic Sattar meta-analysis: OR 1.09, with treatment of 255 individuals for 4 years resulting in 1 additional diabetes case while preventing 5.4 vascular events.[8] The AHA Scientific Statement reports an absolute risk of ~0.2% per year.[6]

**Update the claim should reflect:** The 2024 CTT meta-analysis provides more granular data — the ~9–12% figure applies to moderate-intensity statins, but high-intensity statins carry a substantially higher relative risk (~36%).[7] The absolute excess depends heavily on baseline glycemic status, with ~62% of new diagnoses occurring in individuals already in the top quartile of baseline glycemia.[7] The claim's guidance to continue statins if T2D develops is well-supported.[6][8]

**Claim 11: Statin intolerance/nocebo — SAMSON trial, ~90% nocebo, practical rechallenge approach.**

**Largely supported with nuance.** The SAMSON trial (Howard et al., published in JACC 2021, not NEJM) found a nocebo ratio of **90%** — meaning 90% of the symptom burden attributed to statins was also elicited by placebo.[9] Mean symptom scores were 16.3 on statin, 15.4 on placebo, and 8.0 on no tablet (statin vs. placebo P = 0.388).[9] After the trial, 50% of participants successfully restarted statins.[9]

**Corrections needed:** (a) SAMSON was published in **JACC 2021**, not NEJM 2021. (b) The NLA Clinical Perspective (Warden et al. 2023) cautions that results from SAMSON and StatinWISE "cannot be generalized to all patients" given the small, self-selected populations. (c) The practical rechallenge approach described is consistent with NLA and ACC guidance, though the 2026 guideline does not specify a minimum of "2 statin failures with rechallenge" as a formal threshold — this is a reasonable operational standard but not a guideline-specified number.[9][10] True statin myopathy rates of ~1–10 per 10,000 person-years and rhabdomyolysis ~1–3 per 100,000 are consistent with the AHA Scientific Statement.[6]

**Claim 12: Pregnancy — 2021 FDA labeling change removed categorical contraindication.**

**Supported.** In July 2021, the FDA requested removal of the contraindication against statin use in all pregnant patients, shifting to individualized risk-benefit assessment.[11][1][2] The 2026 guideline states: "In 2021, the FDA changed from providing recommendations for or against taking medicine to recommending individualizing risks and benefits regarding statin use during pregnancy." The 2022 ACC Expert Consensus and 2024 NLA Consensus confirm this.[1][2][11][12]

The claim's characterization that statins "may now be continued in patients with established ASCVD or HeFH on a case-by-case basis" is accurate. The 2026 guideline adds that "it seems prudent to avoid statin therapy during pregnancy and while lactating" for most patients, but statins "may be considered in pregnant persons at very high risk for ASCVD (a history of ASCVD or FH with additional risk factors)."[2] **Note:** Statins remain contraindicated during lactation per FDA labeling.[13]

**Claim 13: HEDIS SUPD measure — ages 40–75 with diabetes, tracked via pharmacy claims, ~90% target exceeds national 90th percentile.**

**Partially verifiable.** The HEDIS SUPD (Statin Use in Persons with Diabetes) measure tracks statin dispensing via pharmacy claims for adults aged 40–75 with diabetes — this is consistent with NCQA specifications. The specific benchmark figures (~80–85% for commercial 90th percentile) and the organizational target of ~90% cannot be independently verified from the medical literature but are plausible based on publicly available NCQA benchmark data.

**Claim 14: HEDIS SUPD aligns with 2026 COR 1 for moderate-intensity statin in diabetes; HEDIS does not distinguish intensity.**

**Supported.** The 2026 guideline recommends moderate-intensity statin for adults 40–75 with diabetes (COR 1, LOE A), with high-intensity for those with multiple ASCVD risk factors (COR 2a).[2] HEDIS SUPD measures any statin dispensing without distinguishing intensity. The claim correctly notes that patients with diabetes and ASCVD or ≥10% 10-year risk qualify for high-intensity statin per the guideline.[2]

The 2026 guideline upgrades this to: "COR 1: In adults at intermediate risk and select adults at borderline risk with no prior ASCVD, if the decision regarding LLT remains uncertain, a CAC score should be used for further risk stratification."[1]

**Claim 1: 2026 ACC/AHA Nonstatin Sequencing Flexibility**

**Largely correct with important nuances.** The 2026 guideline explicitly states that "the revised recommendations no longer require that ezetimibe be added to statin therapy prior to initiating a PCSK9 mAb, and consideration of therapy may be based on degree of LDL-C required and patient preference".[1][2] The guideline lists ezetimibe, PCSK9 mAb, **and/or** bempedoic acid as parallel options (using "or" and "and/or" language) for both VHR ASCVD (COR 1) and non-VHR ASCVD (COR 2a).[2]

**Claim 1: LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL — COR 1 for high-intensity statin, no 10-year risk calculation needed, ≥50% LDL-C reduction goal**

**Supported with corrections.** The 2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guideline (Section 4.2.4.3, Recommendation 2) gives a **COR 1, LOE B-R** for maximally tolerated statin therapy in adults with LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL without calculating 10-year ASCVD risk. - The claim states the recommendation applies to "adults aged 20+" — the 2026 guideline does not specify a lower age bound of 20 for this recommendation. The 2026 guideline simply says "adults" without specifying 20+.
- The claim that "COR 1 means therapy 'should' be provided after SDM, not without discussion" is a reasonable interpretation of ACC/AHA COR definitions, but the 2026 guideline supportive text for this recommendation does not explicitly mandate a formal SDM discussion for the statin initiation itself (unlike the primary prevention pathway for LDL-C 70–189 mg/dL). The SDM framing is more prominent in the primary prevention sections. For LDL-C ≥190, the guideline language is more directive.

**Claim 2: Pre-treatment workup for LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL**

**Supported.** The 2026 guideline (Section 4.2.4.3, Recommendation 1, COR 1, LOE B-NR) explicitly states secondary causes should be excluded. Table 15 of the guideline lists the secondary causes, which include hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, obstructive liver disease/cholestatic disease, CKD, ketogenic/very-low-carb diets, and medications (glucocorticoids, estrogens, cyclosporine, atypical antipsychotics, high-dose thiazides, androgens).
